# How to Establish a Business Bank Account — Fresh Roots Consulting LLC

This guide walks you through opening a business checking account (and optionally savings) in the name of **Fresh Roots Consulting, LLC** so that company income and expenses stay separate from personal finances.

---

## Why a separate business account?

- **Liability and compliance:** Keeps the corporate veil intact and makes it clear the LLC is a distinct entity.
- **Taxes and bookkeeping:** Simplifies tracking income and expenses for Schedule C and your transactions log.
- **Professionalism:** Lets you receive payments and pay vendors in the company name.

---

## Research: Best business bank accounts for small businesses

Below is a summary of options well suited to small businesses and single-member LLCs like Fresh Roots. **You already have personal accounts at Michigan State Credit Union** — starting with their business offering is often the simplest (one login, existing relationship).

### Option 1: Your current credit union — Michigan State Credit Union

If you mean **MSU Federal Credit Union** (msufcu.org):

- **Business membership:** Requires at least one business share account and a one-time $1 non-refundable membership fee. You’ll need your EIN and may need an LLC resolution or signature card for authorized signers.
- **Business checking options** (confirm current terms at [msufcu.org/smallbusinesschecking](https://www.msufcu.org/smallbusinesschecking/) or with a branch):
  - **Basic Business Checking:** Around $10/month (often waived with ~$1,000 minimum daily balance); limited free transactions per month (e.g. 100), then a per-transaction fee; no minimum to open.
  - **Advanced Business Checking:** Higher monthly fee (e.g. $15), often waived with a higher balance (~$5,000); more free transactions; may include a check-order credit.
- **Pros:** One place for personal and business; you’re already a member; local branches if you’re in their service area.
- **Cons:** Fee waivers depend on balance; transaction limits may matter if you have a lot of activity.

If your “Michigan State Credit Union” is a different institution (e.g. another state CU), check their website or call for **business checking** and ask about monthly fees, minimum balance to waive fees, and free transaction limits.

### Option 2: Online / fintech business checking (often $0 fees)

These are frequently recommended for small and solo businesses:

| Provider | Best for | Highlights |
|----------|----------|------------|
| **Bluevine** | LLCs, low activity | $0 monthly fee, $0 min deposit; interest on balances (e.g. up to 1.30% APY on eligible balances); sign-up bonus offers; no overdraft or excess-transaction fees in many cases. |
| **Lili** | Simple tools | $0 monthly fee, $0 min; checking + savings; built-in expense and tax features. |
| **Found** | Single-member LLCs | $0 monthly fee, $0 min; aimed at freelancers and single-member LLCs. |
| **NBKC Bank** | No-fee basics | $0 monthly fee, $0 min; basic and interest-bearing business checking plus savings. |

- **Pros:** No or low fees, no minimum balance, strong apps and often good for bookkeeping.
- **Cons:** No or limited branches; cash deposits may be limited or fee-based; you may need to link an external account (e.g. your credit union) for transfers.

### Option 3: Other credit unions (if you want to compare)

- **Alliant Credit Union** — $0 monthly fee on business checking, interest on balance (e.g. 0.25% APY); membership open with a small donation to a partner org.
- **Digital Federal Credit Union (DCU)** — Free business checking with interest; membership via qualifying group/donation.
- **BECU** — No monthly fee on core business checking; strong digital tools (if you’re in their eligibility area).

Eligibility is often by location, employer, or membership in a partner organization; check each credit union’s website.

### What to compare (quick checklist)

When choosing an account, check:

- **Monthly fee** and how to waive it (e.g. minimum balance, number of transactions).
- **Transaction limits** — free items per month, then per-item fee (especially ACH, debit, wires).
- **Minimum opening deposit** and any ongoing minimum balance.
- **Cash deposits** — free or low-cost options if you take cash.
- **Online banking and app** — bill pay, transfers, mobile check deposit.
- **ATM access** — in-network vs out-of-network fees.
- **Business debit/credit card** — availability and any rewards or fees.

### Suggested approach for Fresh Roots

1. **First:** Call or visit **Michigan State Credit Union** (your current CU) and ask for their current **business checking** terms (fees, minimums, transaction limits). If the monthly fee is waivable with a balance you’re comfortable keeping (e.g. $1,000–$5,000), staying there is often the easiest.
2. **If you want no fees and don’t need a branch:** Open a **Bluevine**, **Lili**, **Found**, or **NBKC** business checking and link it to your personal credit union account for moving money in and out.
3. **If you prefer another credit union:** Use the checklist above and compare **Alliant**, **DCU**, or **BECU** (and any local Michigan CUs) against your MSCU offer.

Rates, fees, and offers change; confirm everything on each institution’s website or with a representative before applying.

## Step-by-step process

### 1. Choose a bank or credit union

- See **Research: Best business bank accounts for small businesses** above for options tailored to your situation (including your current credit union and no-fee online options).
- **Consider:** Monthly fees and how to waive them, minimum balance, transaction limits, branch/ATM access, online banking, and business debit/credit cards.
- **Tip:** Starting with Michigan State Credit Union keeps everything in one place; if you prefer $0 fees and don’t need a branch, an online business account (e.g. Bluevine, Lili, Found) is a strong alternative.

### 2. Confirm business account requirements online

- Go to the bank’s website and find “Business checking” or “Small business.”
- Note: product name, required documents, minimum opening deposit, and any monthly fees or minimum balance.

### 3. Apply (online or in branch)

- **Online:** Use the bank’s business account application; have your documents ready to upload or enter (EIN, business name, address, your SSN as responsible party if asked).
- **In branch:** Bring originals or certified copies of the documents in the checklist above. Tell them you’re opening an account for **Fresh Roots Consulting, LLC** (Michigan LLC).

### 4. Provide business details consistently

Use this exact information so it matches your state filings and Operating Agreement:

| Field | Value |
|-------|--------|
| **Legal business name** | Fresh Roots Consulting, LLC |
| **EIN** | 41-4106328 |
| **Business address** | 1100 S Second Ave, Alpena, MI 49707 |
| **Type of business** | Consulting (e.g. business process and technology efficiency consulting) |
| **Authorized signer / owner** | James Mitterling (sole Member per Operating Agreement) |

### 5. Make the opening deposit and complete setup

- Fund the account with the required minimum (or more).
- Set up online banking and, if offered, business debit card and/or business credit card.
- Order checks in the business name if you need them (optional).

### 6. Connect the account to your bookkeeping

- Use this account for **all** Fresh Roots income (client payments, etc.) and **all** Fresh Roots expenses (subscriptions, fees, contractor payments, etc.).
- Record transactions in **Organization/Transactions/transactions.csv** (and any other system you use) so you have a clear record for taxes.

## After the account is open

- **Do not commingle:** Avoid paying personal expenses from the business account or depositing personal funds except as formal owner contributions or loan repayments per your records.
- **Document authority:** Your Operating Agreement (Article 4.2) already authorizes the Member to open and maintain bank accounts in the company name; keep a signed copy in your records.
- **Update clients and vendors:** Use the new business account details for ACH, wire, or check payments to/from Fresh Roots.
