## Step 1: Choose Your Business Structure

### Common Business Types:
- **Sole Proprietorship** - Simplest, no separate legal entity
- **LLC (Limited Liability Company)** - Recommended for small businesses, provides liability protection
- **Corporation (C-Corp or S-Corp)** - More complex, suitable for larger businesses
- **Partnership** - For multiple owners

**Recommendation:** LLC is typically best for consulting businesses as it provides liability protection with simpler tax treatment.

## Step 2: State of Michigan Filings

### A. Business Name Registration

1. **Name Availability Search**
   - Website: https://www.michigan.gov/corporations/business-entity-search
   - Search your desired business name to ensure it's available
   - Tip: Also check for domain name availability

2. **Reserve Business Name (Optional but Recommended)**
   - File "Application for Reservation of Name"
   - Cost: $25
   - Valid for 6 months
   - Website: https://www.michigan.gov/corporations

### B. Form Your Business Entity

#### For LLC (Recommended):

1. **File Articles of Organization**
   - Website: https://www.michigan.gov/corporations/forms
   - Form: Articles of Organization for Limited Liability Company
   - Filing Fee: $50 (online) or $60 (paper)
   - Online Filing: https://www.michigan.gov/corporations/file-online
   - Required Information:
     - Business name
     - Registered agent name and address
     - Purpose of business
     - Duration (usually perpetual)
     - Management structure
   
   **Registered Agent Requirements:**
   - Must be a Michigan resident or business entity authorized in Michigan
   - Must have a physical Michigan address (P.O. Box not acceptable)
   - Can be yourself or a registered agent service

2. **Create Operating Agreement (Recommended)**
   - Not required by state but highly recommended
   - Defines ownership, management, and operational procedures
   - Keep this document for your records

#### For Corporation:

1. **File Articles of Incorporation**
   - Filing Fee: $60
   - Similar requirements to LLC but more formal structure

## Step 3: Obtain Federal Employer Identification Number (EIN)

### Required For:
- Hiring employees
- Opening business bank accounts
- Filing business taxes
- Most business structures (except sole proprietorships without employees)

### How to Apply:
- **Website:** https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number
- **Cost:** Free
- **Processing:** Immediate (online) or 4-5 weeks (mail/fax)
- **Required Information:**
  - Business name and address
  - Business structure type
  - Reason for applying
  - Responsible party information (SSN or EIN)

**Note:** Apply Monday-Friday, 7am-10pm EST for immediate online processing.

## Step 4: Michigan Business License & Permits

### A. Business Registration with State

1. **Tax Registration**
   - Website: https://www.michigan.gov/taxes
   - Register for business taxes if required
   - May need to register for:
     - Sales tax (if selling taxable goods/services)
     - Use tax
     - Withholding tax (if hiring employees)
     - Single Business Tax (if applicable)

2. **Michigan Business One Stop**
   - Website: https://www.michigan.gov/business
   - Centralized portal for business registration
   - Helps determine required licenses and permits

### B. Professional Licenses (If Applicable)

- Check if your consulting field requires specific licenses
- Website: https://www.michigan.gov/lara/bureau-list/cscl
- Common consulting licenses may include:
  - Professional services licenses
  - Trade licenses
  - Occupational licenses

## Step 5: Local Government Filings

### A. City/Local Business License

1. **Contact Your Local Clerk's Office**
   - Most cities require a business license
   - Varies by municipality
   - Check your city's website or clerk's office

2. **Zoning Compliance**
   - Verify your business location complies with local zoning
   - Home-based businesses may need special permits
   - Contact your local zoning department

### B. Home-Based Business Requirements (If Applicable)

- Check local ordinances
- May require home occupation permit
- Verify if clients can visit your home office

---

## Step 6: Federal Requirements

### A. Business Bank Account

**Required Information:**
- EIN (from Step 3)
- Articles of Organization (from Step 2)
- Operating Agreement (if LLC)
- Personal identification

**Recommended:** Open account at a bank that offers good business banking services

### B. Business Insurance

**Types to Consider:**
- General liability insurance
- Professional liability/errors & omissions insurance (Important for consultants)
- Business property insurance
- Workers' compensation (if hiring employees)

### C. Federal Tax Registration

1. **IRS Business Tax Account**
   - Website: https://www.irs.gov/businesses
   - Determine your tax obligations
   - File required returns

2. **Federal Taxes (Self-Employment)**
   - Quarterly estimated tax payments may be required
   - Website: https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estimated-taxes

---

## Step 7: Ongoing Compliance Requirements

### Annual Filings - Michigan

1. **Annual Report/Annual Statement**
   - Due: February 15th of each year (LLCs and Corporations)
   - Website: https://www.michigan.gov/corporations/file-online
   - Fee: $25 for LLCs, $60 for corporations
   - Can file online or by mail
   - **Important:** Late fees apply if not filed on time

2. **Tax Returns**
   - Michigan Business Tax (if applicable)
   - State income tax
   - Sales/Use tax (if registered)

### Federal Ongoing Requirements

1. **Federal Tax Returns**
   - File annually based on business structure
   - LLC: Usually pass-through (reported on personal return)
   - Corporation: Separate corporate tax return required

2. **Quarterly Estimated Taxes**
   - If you expect to owe $1,000+ in taxes
   - Due: April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15

## Important Deadlines to Remember

1. **Annual Report:** February 15 (Michigan LLCs/Corporations)
2. **Federal Tax Returns:** 
   - C-Corporation: 15th day of 4th month after year-end
   - S-Corporation/Partnership: 15th day of 3rd month after year-end
   - Individual (Sole Prop/LLC): April 15
3. **Estimated Tax Payments:** April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15
4. **Sales Tax Returns:** Based on filing frequency (monthly, quarterly, or annually)

## Helpful Tips

1. **Start with LLC Structure:** Provides liability protection without corporate complexity
2. **Get EIN Even if Not Required:** Makes banking and future expansion easier
3. **Keep Good Records:** Maintain organized records from day one
4. **Consider Professional Help:** Consult with an attorney and/or accountant for complex situations
5. **File Annual Report on Time:** Late fees can add up quickly
6. **Separate Personal and Business Finances:** Essential for liability protection
7. **Register for Taxes Early:** Better to register and find you don't need it than face penalties
